Korea

Korea’s Chuseok comes from ancient harvest and thanksgiving traditions. Families reunite, perform ancestral rites, and visit graves. Songpyeon, a traditional rice cake, is the festival’s most representative food.

China

China’s Mid-Autumn Festival originated from ancient moon-worship and harvest traditions. Today, it emphasizes family reunion, moon-viewing, eating mooncakes, and traditional activities such as moon worship and lantern displays.

Japan

Japan’s Jūgoya moon-viewing tradition came from China over a thousand years ago. People admire the autumn full moon and offer tsukimi dango, pampas grass, and crops, focusing on nature and harvest.

Taiwan

The history of the Mid-Autumn Festival in Taiwan comes from traditional Chinese moon-worship and harvest customs brought by early Chinese settlers. Over time, the festival became an important occasion for families to gather, admire the full moon, and celebrate together. In modern Taiwan, traditions have developed to include eating mooncakes and pomelos, giving gifts, and especially barbecuing with family and friends, which became popular in the late 20th century. Today, the Mid-Autumn Festival in Taiwan combines traditional customs with modern celebrations, focusing on family reunion, food, and spending time together.

Vietnam

Vietnam’s Tết Trung Thu was influenced by Chinese traditions but became a children’s festival. Children enjoy lantern parades, lion dances, and competitions, while families admire the moon and eat mooncakes.
